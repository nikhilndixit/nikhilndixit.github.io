The problem

Customer data was scattered across six disconnected systems, making it hard to understand the end-to-end learner experience. Teams relied on manual exports and cross-functional follow-ups, often spotting problems too late. I took the initiative to scope and build Pulse beyond my core role.

How I shipped it

  1. Phase 1 · Aug–Sep
    Customer rating validation using CSV exports, with data-quality checks for missing or invalid records.
  2. Phase 2 · Sep
    Live data integration via APIs, with standardised records and validated metrics across sources.
  3. Phase 3 · Oct
    Unified learner dashboard connecting profiles, LMS feedback, course progress, marks, and grades.
  4. Phase 4 · Oct
    Cross-source intelligence combining learner metrics with support tickets to uncover recurring pain points and improve product decisions.
  5. Phase 5 · Oct
    Ask Pulse: AI-powered queries that turn learner, program, and cohort data into tailored dashboards and concise summaries.

Product decisions

Ground truth over generated answers

SQL owns the numbers. AI interprets requests and summarises verified results; mismatched figures invalidate the response.

Validate before scaling

Started with CSV uploads to prove value before investing in APIs, automation, and persistent storage.

Evaluate before selecting a model

Defined a 30-prompt evaluation set to test planning accuracy, layout validity, and numerical reliability before choosing a model.

Privacy by design

Uses a local open-source model and masks customer emails before they reach it.

Where it stands

  • Current: End-to-end pilot running locally on real data.
  • Next: Validate model performance, test with internal users, and prepare for hosted deployment.
  • Future: Track actions and outcomes to help Pulse recommend improvements based on past results.

The public repo is a standalone version on synthetic data. It contains no company data, schema or system names. See it on GitHub.

Product · AI Evaluation

LLM Auto-Grader

Automated grading across assignments and projects, reducing grading costs by 80% and overall delivery costs by 20%.

80%Reduction in grading costs
20%Reduction in overall delivery costs
See moreSee less

The problem

Manual grading across courses and programs was expensive, while automated evaluation needed to handle varied rubrics, incomplete submissions, and fabricated content reliably.

My contribution

Led evaluation and testing across diverse assignments and projects, focusing on edge cases, fabricated or incomplete submissions, and data-ingestion issues that could lead to incorrect grades.

Key product decisions

  • Test against realistic and adversarial submissions, not just ideal inputs.
  • Validate data ingestion and completeness before trusting grading outputs.
  • Evaluate across different assignments, rubrics, and programs rather than assuming one test set was sufficient.
